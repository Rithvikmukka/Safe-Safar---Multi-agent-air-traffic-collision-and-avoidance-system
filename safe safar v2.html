01The Motivation & Scale

Modern air travel operates over 105,000+ flights daily with upwards of 20,000 aircraft aloft simultaneously. Centralized human air traffic control is under acute strain: fatigue, understaffing, and missed radio calls accounted for 1,757 near-misses and runway incursions in 2024 alone.

Safe Safar decentralizes decision-making. Instead of one overburdened ground controller commanding every heading, each aircraft operates as an autonomous rational agent. It projects its forward trajectory, communicates directly with approaching peers, and calculates collision-free detours in real time.

02Agent Formulation — PEAS Model

Before implementing search and avoidance logic, each aircraft agent is framed according to the formal PEAS model:

Performance Zero collisions, enforce minimum safe separation (>34 NM), minimize fuel burn, minimize detour distance and delay, reach assigned destination.
Environment Dynamic, continuous 2D/flight-level airspace graph with aeronautical waypoint fixes (KODEX, ELKEY, etc.), variable traffic density, and active flight paths.
Actuators Waypoint heading vector changes, cruise speed throttling, flight level adjustments, local A* replanning, and holding pattern execution.
Sensors Internal telemetry (position, speed, fuel reserve, destination goal) and forward radar transponder sensing nearby aircraft within its forward perception horizon.
Cost = (CollisionRisk × LargePenalty) + wd·Distance + wf·Fuel + wt·Delay The collision penalty term strictly dominates all others — guaranteeing that no rational agent ever selects a risky shortcut for fuel or schedule savings.

04How Each Agent Decides: A* & Peer Negotiation

1. Airspace as a Waypoint Graph: Navigational fixes are laid out across sectors. Flight paths are formulated as path-search queries over this graph using A* Informed Search with Euclidean straight-line distance as an admissible heuristic $h(n) \le h^*(n)$:

f(n) = g(n) + h(n) g(n) is the accumulated flight distance from origin; h(n) is the straight-line distance to destination. Expanding the lowest f(n) guarantees an optimal path.

2. Forward Temporal Conflict Projection: Every tick, each agent projects its own and nearby aircraft's coordinates along future time horizons $t \in \{0, 2, 4, 6, 8\}\,\text{seconds}$.

IF distance(predicted_A(t), predicted_B(t)) < SafetyRadius THEN Trigger Peer-to-Peer Conflict Protocol

3. The 5 Core Conflict Resolution Cases:

A
Pairwise Conflict (Head-On / Crossing)The lower-priority aircraft yields and executes local penalized A* replanning around the conflict zone; the higher-priority aircraft maintains course.
B

05Decision-Making Parameters & Mathematical Engine

In Safe Safar’s decentralized multi-agent system, autonomous aircraft make safety-critical tactical navigation decisions without a centralized air traffic controller. Every decision—whether an aircraft maintains course, yields, diverts via an A* avoidance path, or enters an emergency loiter—is governed by a deterministic mathematical framework.

2. The Core Mathematical Formulas

The decentralized decision loop operates through five interconnected mathematical equations:

Equation 01 · Priority Negotiation
Agent Priority Score Formulation
Pi = (1 - Fi / Fmax,i) × 60 + 0.4 × wp,i

Quantifies an aircraft's right-of-way in peer-to-peer encounters. Composed of two weighted terms:

  • Fuel Urgency Term: (1 - F/Fmax) × 60 scales from 0 (full tank) to 60 (empty tank).
  • Mission Weight Term: 0.4 × wp contributes up to 40 points based on flight category.
  • Safety Guarantee: An aircraft with under 15% fuel produces a score of ~51+ points, guaranteeing that fuel-depleted aircraft never yield to full-tank peers, regardless of scheduled flight weights.
Equation 02 · Trajectory Projection
Temporal Separation & Conflict Test
Δr(t) = [pA + vA·t] - [pB + vB·t]
Conflict ⇔ mint∈[0, 8s] ||Δr(t)|| < Dsafety

Projects 2D spatial positions forward at 2-second intervals along each aircraft's instantaneous flight vector:

  • Δr(t): Relative displacement vector between Aircraft A and B at future time t.
  • Trigger Condition: If ||Δr(t)|| < 34 NM for any t ∈ {0, 2, 4, 6, 8} seconds AND aircraft are within mutual radar coverage (dist ≤ 280 NM), a P2P Conflict Hotspot is triggered.
Equation 03 · Peer Negotiation
Right-of-Way & Deterministic Tie-Breaking
YieldDecision(A, B) =
• If PA > PB + ε ⇒ Flight B yields (A* detour)
• If PB > PA + ε ⇒ Flight A yields (A* detour)
• If |PA - PB| ≤ ε ⇒ min(IDA, IDB) yields

Eliminates communication deadlocks with zero round-trip overhead:

  • Hysteresis Margin (ε = 0.6): Prevents decision flapping when priority scores are nearly identical.
  • Deterministic Symmetry Breaking: When priority scores match exactly (e.g. Case D preset), the lower unique integer ID yields automatically without central coordination.
Equation 04 · Path Replanning
Penalized A* Cost Function
f(n) = g(n) + h(n) + ∑k [ K / max(1, dist(n, pconflict,k)2) ]
where h(n) = ||pn - pgoal||

The yielding aircraft replans around the conflict hotspot across the navigational graph:

  • g(n): Path distance from current position to waypoint n.
  • h(n): Admissible straight-line Euclidean distance to destination fix (guarantees optimality when unobstructed).
  • Repulsion Potential Field: Injects an inverse-square penalty into graph vertices near the conflict point, bending the optimal route outward.
Equation 05 · Fail-Safe & Performance
Holding Pattern Loiter & Global PEAS Cost
phold(t) = [xfix + r·cos(ωt), yfix + r·sin(ωt)]
J(π) = (CollisionRisk × 106) + wd·Dist + wf·ΔF + wt·Δt

Fail-safe contingency and overarching rational agent utility:

  • Holding Contingency (Case C): If all graph neighbors are constrained by conflict penalties, the aircraft loiters at reduced speed around waypoint fix [xfix, yfix] until corridors clear.
  • Dominant Penalty (106): The collision risk penalty strictly dominates route efficiency, guaranteeing 100% safety separation as the highest objective.
